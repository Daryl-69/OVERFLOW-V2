        The first-glass card

        A5, printed at 100%. The four corner squares let the phone undo the angle; the checkerboard under the glass blurs as water gets cloudier; the strip pad sits next to the printed chlorine scale so both are seen under the same light. The QR code carries only the household ID.

        How Upstream finds the crack

        In cities where water runs an hour or two a day, sewage seeps into emptied pipes between supplies. When the water comes back, the front picks up that dirty slug and carries it downstream. Homes just downstream get dirty first water; homes farther on get it more diluted; homes upstream never see it. Seismologists locate an earthquake from when its waves reach each station. Upstream does the same with first glasses of water.

        What runs underneath

        • Reader. Corner markers fix the angle; printed patches fix the light; the checkerboard's contrast loss gives cloudiness; the strip pad is placed on the printed chlorine scale.
        • Levels. Green, amber or red against each tap's own baseline and the 0.2 mg/L chlorine minimum (IS 10500).
        • Velocity model. Normal-day photo times give each pipe's travel time and the direction water runs round loops — or rebuild a missing pipe map.
        • Triangulation. Every 20 m pipe piece is scored against every photo, averaging over plausible flow directions, so a few pranks barely move it.
        • Outputs. A dig spot with its evidence, a personal safe-after time for each downstream home, and which homes should test next.

        Upstream is an early-warning and leak-finding tool, not a safety certificate. Red alerts still go to lab confirmation.
